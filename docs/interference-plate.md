# Interference Plate

A visual philosophy for still images made alongside this site (share card, icon, any printed piece).

## The movement

A hologram is not a picture of a thing. It is a record of how two waves disagreed about it. Interference Plate treats every surface as that record: a dark, patient ground on which two wavefronts meet, cancel and reinforce, and from whose fringes a subject can be given back. The work is never illustrative. It is evidence, the kind of image an instrument produces when it is left running in a quiet room, and it must look as though someone at the very top of their craft tuned that instrument for weeks before allowing a single exposure.

## Space and form

The ground is deep and almost empty, a blue-black that reads as depth rather than as a flat fill. Across it run hairline fringes, families of hyperbolae generated by exactly two sources and nothing else. Their geometry is strict: no ornament, no freehand curve, no line that the two sources could not have produced. Density does the work that decoration usually does. Where fringes crowd, the eye slows; where they open, it rests. Form emerges from accumulation, hundreds of marks each placed by the same rule, the result of meticulous, painstaking calculation rather than gesture.

## Colour and material

Light is the only pigment. A single cold beam, cyan leaning to mint, carries the subject; the full diffraction spectrum appears only where a real foil would show it, at grazing angles and on edges, as thin as a scratch. Colour is never spread to fill an area. It is dispersed, the way a prism disperses, and it is always attached to a line or a contour. Black is never black: it is ink with distance in it. Every tone is calibrated against its neighbours with the care of a master printer pulling proofs.

## Scale and rhythm

Two scales only, far apart. One element is monumental, set so large it becomes architecture: a name, a figure, a single form. Everything else is minute: registration ticks, coordinate labels, a measurement in a dot-matrix face. Nothing sits in between. The gap between the two scales is the composition's tension, and the tiny marks reward a second look the way the margins of a scientific plate do.

## Composition and hierarchy

The subject stands off-centre, in front of the type, lit from within, partly occluding what is behind it so that depth is real rather than drawn. Text is rare and clinical: a name, a role, two coordinates. Margins are generous and exact; nothing touches an edge it was not designed to bleed through, and nothing crowds anything else. The finished piece should feel inevitable, as if no element could move a millimetre without the fringes changing, the mark of deep expertise and of countless hours spent refining what was already there instead of adding more.
